# Short-drama craft

Chinese vertical drama / ReelShort style, adapted to Thai viewers.

## Structure of every episode

- **Hook in the first 3 seconds:** open with a shocking line or an unfair situation already happening. Never open with greetings, weather or setup.
- **One beat per scene:** a choice, a reveal or a reversal. Something must change every 15-20 seconds.
- **Spring and release:** compress the spring (humiliation, injustice, the antagonist's absurd logic), then release it (a face-slapping comeback, proof, a reveal). Put the most heated confrontation around the middle, then the reversal.
- **End on a cliffhanger** (except the finale): an open question that makes people wait for the next EP.
- **The finale** gives the biggest payoff of the style: the antagonist finally loses and apologises, everyone sees the truth, every open thread closes.

## Characters

- The antagonist is certain they are right; viewers must want to yell at the screen. Give them the personality the style asks for: loud and absurd, two-faced and smooth, or crying on cue. Let them say their absurd lines themselves.
- The hero narrates as "ผม", deadpan and a bit sarcastic by default, but follow the style: playing dumb, sly, irritable but rule-perfect, playful. Not the same calm face every series.
- The hero wins with brains, evidence, skill or the law. Never with violence.
- Side characters get hurt by the antagonist's antics and slowly turn against them.

## Lines

- Short and sharp. No filler, no explaining the obvious, no line said twice.
- Mix narration (the hero talking to the viewer) and dialogue (characters speaking). At least 40% dialogue.
- Dialogue is written as the person would say it out loud: no name prefix, no quotes.
- When the style teaches something (food, repair, law), include ONE short, true, interesting fact per episode that the audience learns from.

## Thai realism

- Everyday Thai places: markets, shop houses, condos, offices, villages, food stalls, garages, police stations, district offices.
- Money in baht, LINE chats, Facebook groups, TikTok lives, Shopee orders, Traffy Fondue, the consumer protection board.
- Phone screens (chats, reviews, lives, posts, documents) are 20-30% of the shots: they are cheap to produce and keep the story moving.

## Staging (makes the animation believable)

- People DO something while they talk: cook, count money, repair, sweep, sign, carry, pay, film with a phone. At most 1 in 3 people per scene just stands.
- A shop owner stands behind the counter, an officer behind the desk, customers in front.
- Film it like one continuous take: keep the same place and people for a whole conversation; change place only when the story moves.
- Max 3 characters per scene.

## Platform package per EP

- **Title / caption:** starts with `EPn`, max 70 characters, makes people curious, no spoiler of the twist.
- **Hashtags:** 3-6, Thai and English mix, relevant to the story and the genre.
- **Teaser:** max 60 characters, a juicy spoiler of the next EP shown as text at the end.
- **Ask:** one spoken question to the viewers that makes them comment, e.g. ถ้าเป็นคุณจะให้อภัยมันไหม.
- **Cover text:** max 22 characters, the series title or the episode's hottest phrase.
